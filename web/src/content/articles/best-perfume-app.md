Most people go looking for a perfume app at the same moment. You are standing in front of the shelf, you own more bottles than you can keep straight, and you have just realized you bought something that smells a lot like a bottle already sitting there. Or the Notes app entry you started two years ago has forty lines in it and you have not opened it since March.

This is the honest version of that comparison. We build one of the apps on this list, so read the Perfume Picks section with that in mind. We have tried to be specific about where it is the wrong choice.

Everything below was checked against the US App Store on 31 August 2026. Ratings and version numbers move, so verify anything that matters to you on the listing itself.

## What does a perfume app actually need to do?

Four jobs, and almost no app does more than two of them well.

1. **Look a bottle up.** Notes, accords, the year it came out, whether the reformulation ruined it, what a thousand strangers thought.
2. **Hold your collection.** What you own, what is open, what is nearly empty, what you paid.
3. **Recommend something to buy.** Based on what you already like.
4. **Tell you what to wear today.** From the bottles on your own shelf, not from a catalog.

Job one is crowded and mature. Job four barely exists. The two in the middle are where the apps actually differ, and where most people end up disappointed, because they downloaded a research tool and expected it to manage a shelf.

## Fragrantica: still the reference, still not an app

Fragrantica is where the fragrance internet goes to look something up. Note pyramids, reformulation arguments, the long comment threads where somebody has owned three different batches of the same bottle and can tell you which year it changed.

Worth knowing before you go looking: **there is no official Fragrantica app.** Searching the US App Store in August 2026 returns third-party clients built by independent developers, the most polished of which is Fragram at 4.70 stars across 1,432 ratings. None of them are published by Fragrantica. The site itself works fine on a phone browser, which is how most people use it.

**Use it for:** deciding whether to buy a bottle, understanding what is actually in one, finding out whether the version on the shelf is the good version.

**Do not expect it to:** hold your collection, remember what you wore, or work offline.

## Parfumo: the deepest catalog you can put in your pocket

Parfumo is German-built and has become the serious alternative to Fragrantica, with one advantage that matters daily: it ships a real app, and a good one. At 4.87 stars across 749 US ratings, updated to version 5.5.5 on 20 August 2026, it is the highest-rated app in this comparison by a distance.

The database is the draw. It also lets you manage a collection and check in whatever you are wearing, which puts it halfway into job two.

Where it stops short is the wear log. Check-ins record that you wore something. They do not build up into the thing most collectors eventually want, which is a picture of what you actually reach for versus what you told yourself you would reach for when you bought it.

**Use it for:** lookup, collection cataloging, and community scores, all in one app.

**Do not expect it to:** tell you what to wear, or do much with your collection beyond listing it.

## Aromoshelf: the closest thing to a direct competitor

Aromoshelf calls itself an AI Scent Wardrobe and is the app most similar in intent to ours. 4.77 stars across 349 US ratings, version 3.81.2, updated 28 August 2026, so it is actively developed. It organizes what you own, tracks your wear history, and helps you plan what to buy next.

We are not going to pretend to be a neutral judge of it. If you want a collection tracker with a longer track record and a much larger body of reviews than ours, it is the obvious one to try first, and trying both costs nothing.

## PERFUMIST: a recommendation engine, not a shelf

PERFUMIST is built around one question: what should I buy? Its own listing claims over 50,000 perfumes from nearly 2,000 brands, and the app ranks them against a profile you build. 4.40 stars across 545 US ratings, though the last update was 15 April 2026, so it is on a slower cycle than the others.

It is genuinely good at the shopping question. It is not built to manage what is already on your shelf, and the recommendations point outward, toward things to buy, rather than inward, toward the eight bottles you own and have not worn since spring.

## Perfume Picks: for the bottle you already paid for

Disclosure, again: we publish this article and we build this app.

Perfume Picks is iOS only, free to download, currently version 1.0.7. The catalog holds 13,188 fragrances across 764 houses as of today. The premise is narrow on purpose. Discovery is a solved problem and four other apps on this list solve it. The question nobody was answering is what to wear on a Tuesday from the bottles already in the room.

So the app reads your taste from bottles you recognize, then hands you a pick each morning that accounts for the weather and for what you have worn recently, out of your own wardrobe. Every wear you log ticks a cost-per-wear counter against the purchase price, which has an uncomfortable habit of revealing that the expensive bottle you wear constantly is the cheap one, and the niche purchase you have worn three times is not.

**Strong at:** wear logging, cost-per-wear, a daily pick from your own shelf, finding [cheaper bottles that smell close](/articles/how-to-find-similar-perfumes) to something you love.

**Not the right answer for:** Android users, anyone who wants community reviews before buying (there is no review feed), and anyone shopping on ratings volume, where we lose to everything else here.

**The free tier is genuinely limited, and we would rather say so than have you find out:** five bottles in the wardrobe, five scans total, ten wear logs, three taste swipes a day. Pro is $2.99 a month or $24.99 a year with a seven-day trial. If your collection is bigger than five bottles, which it probably is if you are reading a comparison of perfume apps, you will hit the cap quickly.

## Can any app tell you if a perfume is fake?

This comes up constantly in searches and the honest answer is no, so it is worth spending a paragraph on rather than dodging.

No app can authenticate a bottle from a photo, and an app that claims to can should make you more suspicious, not less. What actually identifies a counterfeit is physical: the fit and weight of the cap, the quality of the sprayer and the mist it throws, the color of the juice, the print quality on the box, and the batch code. A batch code checker will decode the production date, and a date that cannot be reconciled with the packaging is a real signal. It is a clue, not a verdict, since the codes on good fakes are copied from real bottles.

The defense that works is boring. Buy from an authorized retailer or a large one with a returns policy, and be suspicious of a price that is well under everyone else's.

## What about Scentbird and the subscription apps?

Scentbird's app has 4.85 stars across roughly 86,000 US ratings, which dwarfs every other app in this comparison. It is not really competing with them. It is the front end for a decant subscription, so the app exists to manage your queue and your billing.

If your question is which subscription to join, that is a different article and a different decision. If your question is which app helps you handle the bottles you already own, a subscription app is not the answer, and the ratings count is measuring the popularity of the service rather than the quality of the software.

## Why not just use Notes or a spreadsheet?

Because you already are, and you are reading this anyway.

A note works for the first ten bottles. Below that you can hold the collection in your head and the note is a backup. Somewhere past twenty it quietly breaks, which is the point most people start [auditing the collection properly](/articles/how-to-audit-your-perfume-collection): you stop adding new bottles for a few weeks, then the ones you finished are still listed, then you are not sure whether the entry for a bottle reflects the current formulation or the one you bought in 2023. Nobody decides to abandon the note. It just goes stale and then it is wrong, which is worse than not having it.

The friction is what kills it, not the missing features. Any app on this list beats a spreadsheet only because logging a wear takes one tap instead of finding the row.

## How to choose, in two questions

**What do you mostly need it for?**

- Looking bottles up before buying: Fragrantica in a browser, or Parfumo if you want an app.
- Cataloging what you own: Parfumo, Aromoshelf, or Perfume Picks.
- Being told what to buy next: PERFUMIST.
- Being told what to wear today: Aromoshelf or Perfume Picks.

**Do you log what you wear, honestly, most days?**

If yes, you want a wear log, and that narrows it to Aromoshelf or Perfume Picks. If no, and you are being truthful with yourself about that, do not pay for a tracker. Use Parfumo, keep the collection list current, and you will get most of the value.

Running two apps is normal and most collectors we hear from do it: one for research, one for the shelf. Nobody has managed to do both jobs well in one app yet, ours included.

## What changes from here

Three things we expect to shift.

**Scanning a bottle will stop being a feature and start being the baseline.** Typing a fragrance name into a search box is the single biggest reason people stop cataloging. Any app still requiring manual entry in two years will lose the people with large collections.

**The daily-wear question will get crowded.** Right now very few apps try to answer it. It is the most-asked question a collector has and the least-served, so that will not last.

**Ratings volume is going to keep being a bad signal.** Scentbird has 86,000 ratings for a billing app. We have six for a wear tracker. Neither number tells you much about whether the software solves your problem, and we would rather you tried two apps for a week than trusted a star average.

## What we left out

- **Basenotes** has a first-party app on the App Store, but with a single US rating there is not enough there to judge yet.
- **Sephora, Ulta and retailer apps** are shopping apps with a fragrance section. Fine for buying, useless for collecting.
- **Generic trackers** like Notion and Airtable technically work and some people love them. If you enjoy building the system, build it. Most people want the system to already exist.
