## Why is Perfume Picks the best perfume app for most collectors?

Most perfume apps answer "what is this bottle?" Perfume Picks answers "what should I wear today?" and "which bottle is worth buying next?", and it answers both from your own wardrobe.

- **Fragrance DNA.** You tap up to five bottles you know and love, and the app builds your taste profile from their accords. It sharpens every time you log a wear or swipe in Train My Nose.
- **Scent of the Day.** Each day the app picks a bottle from your own wardrobe, using your taste profile, the local weather, the occasion and what you haven't worn lately, and tells you why. You can log the wear in one tap.
- **Wear log.** Each wear records the date, the occasion, the weather and a rating, and the bottle's remaining-mL meter drops with it, so you can see which bottles you reach for and which are running low.
- **Dupes.** Open any bottle and you see its cheaper dupes ranked by how close they smell, with what you'd save, usually 30 to 70 percent. Free shows the closest one and Pro unlocks the rest.
- **Label scanning.** Point the camera at a bottle and it goes in your wardrobe. You can also search the catalog, which holds more than 13,000 fragrances from 767 brands.

Perfume Picks is free to download on iPhone. The free version holds five bottles in the wardrobe, five label scans, ten wear logs and three Train My Nose swipes a day. Pro is $2.99 a month or $24.99 a year, with a seven-day free trial. It removes those limits, unlocks the full dupes list, and adds Perfume Wrapped, a yearly recap built from your own wears.

There's no Android version, and the pool of reviews is small next to Parfumo's. If reading other people's reviews before you buy is what you want, Parfumo covers that.

## Parfumo

Parfumo is German-built and has the deepest catalog you can put in your pocket. It has 4.89 stars from 1,287 US ratings, and version 5.8.7 came out on 23 November 2026, the highest-rated app in this comparison by a distance. You can manage a collection and check in whatever you're wearing. The check-ins record that you wore something, but they don't build into a picture of what you reach for versus what you told yourself you'd reach for when you bought it.

Parfumo is the one to open when you want to read about a bottle before you buy it. It doesn't pick what to wear.

## Aromoshelf

Aromoshelf calls itself an AI Scent Wardrobe and is the app closest to Perfume Picks in intent. It has 4.77 stars from 392 US ratings, and version 3.85.6 came out on 21 November 2026, so it's actively developed. It organizes what you own, tracks your wear history, and helps you plan what to buy next. It has a longer track record and many more reviews than Perfume Picks, and trying both costs nothing.

## PERFUMIST

PERFUMIST is built around one question: what should I buy? Its listing claims over 50,000 perfumes from nearly 2,000 brands, ranked against a profile you build. It has 4.40 stars from 548 US ratings, and the last update was 15 April 2026, so it's on a slower cycle than the others. It's good at the shopping question. It isn't built to manage what's already on your shelf, and its recommendations point at things to buy rather than at the bottles you own and haven't worn since spring.

## Fragrantica

Fragrantica is where the fragrance internet goes to look something up: note pyramids, reformulation arguments, and comment threads where someone has owned three batches of the same bottle and can tell you which year it changed. There is no official Fragrantica app. Searching the US App Store in November 2026 returns apps built by independent developers, the most polished being Fragram at 4.69 stars from 1,717 ratings, and none of them are published by Fragrantica. The site works on a phone browser, which is how most people use it.

## What about Scentbird?

Scentbird's app has 4.85 stars from about 90,000 US ratings, more than every other app here combined. It's the front end for a decant subscription, so the app manages your queue and your billing. If your question is which subscription to join, that's a different decision. If your question is which app helps with the bottles you already own, a subscription app isn't it.

## Can any app tell you if a perfume is fake?

No. No app can authenticate a bottle from a photo, and an app that claims to should make you more suspicious. What identifies a counterfeit is physical: the fit and weight of the cap, the quality of the sprayer and the mist it throws, the color of the juice, the print quality on the box, and the batch code. A batch code checker decodes the production date, and a date that can't be reconciled with the packaging is a real signal, though good fakes copy codes from real bottles. Buy from an authorized retailer, or a large one with a returns policy, and be suspicious of a price well under everyone else's.

## Why not just use Notes or a spreadsheet?

A note works for the first ten bottles. Somewhere past twenty it goes stale: the bottles you finished are still listed, and you're not sure whether an entry reflects the current formulation or the one you bought in 2023. That's usually the point people start [auditing the collection properly](/articles/how-to-audit-your-perfume-collection). Any app on this list beats a note because logging a wear takes one tap instead of finding the row.

- If you want a daily pick from your own wardrobe, a wear log and a dupe finder, go with [Perfume Picks](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/perfume-picks/id6774184221).
